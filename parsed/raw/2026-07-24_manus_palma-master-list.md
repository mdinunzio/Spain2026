# The Ultimate Palma Master List: 80+ Highly-Rated Locations

To help you decide exactly where to base yourself, I have mapped out over 80 highly-rated, real locations across Palma. They are organized primarily by neighborhood so you can see exactly where the highest density of your preferred activities lies. 

For your travel style—walking out the door and being in the midst of great food, cafes, and independent boutiques—the two main contenders are **Santa Catalina** (bohemian, foodie-focused, slightly more relaxed) and the **Old Town/Center** (historic, dense with sights, high-end shopping, and fine dining).

***

## 1. Santa Catalina & Es Jonquet
*This is the undisputed food and brunch capital of Palma. It is a former fishermen's district that has evolved into a vibrant, highly walkable neighborhood packed with independent boutiques, cocktail bars, and international dining. The adjacent Es Jonquet area is famous for its historic windmills and cobblestone streets.*

### Cafes & Brunch
*   **SĀNTAL Café:** A hidden gem located under a historic windmill, offering a unique setting for specialty coffee [1].
*   **Mama Carmen's Coffee & Kitchen:** A very popular vegetarian café known for exceptional muesli bowls and a great outdoor plaza seating area [2].
*   **El Perrito:** Widely considered the best brunch spot in the neighborhood, located right across from the market [3].
*   **Batx:** A stylish, plant-filled café serving specialty coffee and signature bagels (especially the smoked salmon) [2].

### Lunch & Casual Dining
*   **Mercat de Santa Catalina:** The neighborhood's beating heart. Grab a quick lunch at **Bar Joan Frau** or excellent dim sum at **Arume Sushi Bar** right at the market counters [3].
*   **Bistro Esencia:** A small, romantic spot offering top-class dining away from the busier party crowds [1].

### Dinner (Upscale & Romantic)
*   **Stagier:** A tiny, incredibly romantic Chilean-Peruvian fusion restaurant that is perfect for date nights [3].
*   **Izakaya High Japanese Cuisine:** Consistently rated as one of the best dining experiences in the neighborhood [4].

### Cocktail Bars & Rooftops
*   **Hostal Cuba Sky Bar:** The quintessential Palma sunset spot. Head to this rooftop between 6:00 PM and 7:00 PM for drinks with sweeping views of the marina and cathedral [5].
*   **Aga Bar:** A cozy, intimate bar specializing in high-end agave spirits (mezcal and tequila) [6].
*   **Clandestino Cocktail Club:** A quirky, relaxed speakeasy tucked away on a quiet side street [6].
*   **Idem Bar:** A moody, relaxed neighborhood gem perfect for a nightcap [5].

### Sights & Walks
*   **Es Jonquet Windmills:** Walk through this historic, protected fishermen's village right next to Santa Catalina, known for its old windmills and street art [4].
*   **Es Baluard Museum of Modern Art:** Located on the edge of the neighborhood, featuring contemporary art and a fantastic rooftop terrace [4].

***

## 2. The Old Town (La Calatrava, Sant Jaume, Sant Nicolau)
*The historic center is characterized by narrow, winding cobblestone streets, Gothic architecture, and hidden courtyards. This is where you will find the highest concentration of Michelin-starred restaurants, historic sights, and luxury shopping.*

### Cafes & Brunch
*   **Mistral Coffee House:** Located on Plaça Weyler, this is arguably the most sought-after specialty coffee roaster in Mallorca [2].
*   **Can Joan de s'Aigo:** Established in 1700, this is the oldest chocolatier in Europe. Stop here for traditional ensaimadas and almond granizado [1].
*   **Arabay:** A massive flagship café and roastery near Plaza Mayor, perfect for serious coffee enthusiasts [1].
*   **Café Rialto:** Located inside the Rialto Living store, offering healthy brunches and fresh juices in a stunning Mediterranean courtyard [1].

### Lunch & Casual Tapas
*   **La Rosa Vermutería:** A wildly popular vermouth bar serving giant tortilla pintxos and excellent charcuterie [3].
*   **Toque de Queda:** Set in a converted ancient bakery, serving Italian-Mallorcan small plates and excellent cava [3].
*   **Bar España:** A high-energy pintxos bar tucked down a side street near Plaza Mayor [3].
*   **Bodega La Rambla:** An old-school tavern operating since the 1940s, famous for its traditional Mallorcan *variat* (mixed tapas platter) [3].

### Dinner (Fine Dining & Upscale)
*   **DINS Santi Taura:** A 1-Michelin-star restaurant offering an 11-course tasting menu focused purely on Mallorcan heritage [7].
*   **Marc Fosh:** A 1-Michelin-star restaurant housed in a 17th-century monastery, known for its elegant tasting menus [3].
*   **Adrián Quetglas:** A Michelin-starred spot offering a highly imaginative 8-course tasting menu [4].
*   **El Camino:** A bustling, high-end tapas bar with a long counter and open kitchen, created by the team behind London's Barrafina [3].

### Wine Bars & Cocktails
*   **Brassclub:** An upscale cocktail bar with a 1950s New York aesthetic, run by an award-winning mixologist [6].
*   **Bar Ábaco:** A theatrical, baroque mansion filled with fruit pyramids, classical music, and candles. It is an expensive but highly unique experience [6].
*   **De Tokio a Lima Bar:** A stylish rooftop bar above Paseo del Borne serving Japanese-Peruvian cocktails [5].
*   **El Llorenç Rooftop (Ç):** Boasts the longest infinity pool in Palma with incredible views over the bay and cathedral [8].

### Sights & Architecture
*   **Palma Cathedral (La Seu):** The iconic Gothic masterpiece overlooking the water, featuring interior modifications by Antoni Gaudí.
*   **Royal Palace of La Almudaina:** The historic Moorish-era royal residence located right next to the cathedral.
*   **Arab Baths (Banys Àrabs):** 10th-century Moorish baths set within a tranquil garden courtyard.
*   **Basilica de Sant Francesc:** A stunning 13th-century Gothic church with a beautiful, peaceful cloister.

### Shopping
*   **Paseo del Borne:** The primary luxury shopping boulevard, home to brands like Loewe and Louis Vuitton.
*   **Avenida Jaime III:** A major high-end shopping street featuring both international brands and local designers.
*   **Carrer de Sant Feliu:** A charming street lined with independent artists' studios and offbeat boutiques [9].
*   **Rialto Living:** A massive, beautifully curated lifestyle concept store offering fashion, books, and interior design [1].
*   **Paula's Room:** A chic, independent women's clothing boutique on Carrer de Sant Nicolau [10].

***

## 3. The Surrounding Neighborhoods (Walkable or Short Taxi)

### The "Coffee Corridor" (Near La Rambla / Arxiduc)
*   **Café Riutort:** An urban, Berlin-style café with a fantastic vegetarian menu and community feel [2].
*   **La Molienda:** The pioneers of Palma's specialty coffee scene, known for their flat whites and local ingredients [1].
*   **Bar La Sang:** A brilliant natural wine bar serving earthy Mallorcan reds alongside tapas like burrata and anchovies [3].

### Portixol (Coastal Walk East of Center)
*   **The Vibe:** A former fishing village that has transformed into a trendy coastal suburb. It is perfect for a long, flat walk along the Mediterranean promenade, lined with modern cafes and seafood restaurants [1].

### West of Center (Hikes & Views)
*   **Bellver Castle:** A unique 14th-century circular castle sitting on a pine-covered hill just west of the city.
*   **Bellver Forest Walk:** A relatively easy, scenic hike through the pines up to the castle, offering panoramic views of the entire Bay of Palma.

***

## 4. Activities (Accessible from Anywhere in Palma)

### Beach Clubs (10-15 min taxi)
*   **Purobeach Illetas:** A premium beach club set on tiered cliffs with panoramic views and DJ sets [11].
*   **Balneario Illetas:** A massive, gastronomy-focused beach club located directly on the sand [11].

### Boat Charters (Walkable from Center)
*   **SailPalma:** Operates out of La Lonja Marina (central Palma). They offer private half-day catamaran charters with tapas, paddleboards, and a dedicated crew [12].

### Cooking Classes
*   **Soqueta Experiences:** The top-rated cooking class in the city. It includes a guided tour of the Mercat de l'Olivar followed by a private cooking session in a family kitchen [13].
