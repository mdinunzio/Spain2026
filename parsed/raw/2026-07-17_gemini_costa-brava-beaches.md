The beach opportunities in the Costa Brava are world-class, but they offer a
completely different experience than the powdery white sand you will find in
Santanyí.

This is exactly why splitting the trip between the two regions is so brilliant:
Santanyí delivers the classic, lay-on-a-towel-all-day beach experience, while
the Costa Brava delivers the dramatic, exploratory, culinary beach experience.

Here is exactly what the beach landscape looks like in the Costa Brava and how
to best experience it:

## The Geography: Think Amalfi, but Wilder
The beaches here are called calas (coves). They are carved into the rocky
cliffs, often surrounded by thick pine forests that grow right down to the
water's edge.

- **The Sand:** It is typically coarse, golden sand or small, smooth pebbles.
- **The Water:** Because of the rocky shoreline, the water clarity is
  unbelievable. It is deep, emerald-green, and perfect for snorkeling.
- **The Access:** Many of the best calas are connected by the Camí de Ronda
  coastal hiking path. You will literally be walking through a pine forest on a
  cliff and suddenly look down to see a hidden, pristine beach.

## The "Beach Club" Vibe: Gastronomic Luxury
The Costa Brava does not do the massive, tiered, Ibiza-style beach clubs with
DJs, infinity pools, and bottle service. The luxury here is entirely culinary.

Instead of a plush daybed, you are securing a highly coveted reservation at a
rustic chiringuito (beach restaurant) sitting literally on the sand.

You will hike down to a beach like Aiguablava or Sa Tuna, take a swim in the
ocean, and then sit down to a massive, two-hour lunch of whole fish roasted
over an oak-wood fire. It perfectly aligns with clean, high-protein eating
while offering some of the best coastal dining in Europe.

## The Private Boat Hack (The Croatian Yacht Alternative)
Since you loved the frictionless experience of chartering a yacht with your
friends in Croatia, you can effortlessly replicate a mini-version of that here.

The absolute best way to experience the Costa Brava beaches is from the water.
You can hire a small private boat with a skipper out of a town like Llafranc or
Palamós for the day.

Your skipper can take you to hidden coves that are completely inaccessible by
land, allowing you to swim and lounge in total privacy, far away from any
crowds, before dropping you at a coastal village for a Michelin-starred dinner.

## Top Calas to Have on Your Radar (Near Begur):

- **Cala Sa Tuna:** A tiny, incredibly picturesque cove surrounded by old
  fishermen's houses with colorful doors. It has a fantastic, quiet tavern
  right on the water.
- **Cala Aiguablava:** One of the few calas with finer sand. The water here is
  famously bright blue, and it is home to the restaurant Toc al Mar.
- **Cala s'Alguer:** A 16th-century settlement with no commercial development.
  It is visually stunning, incredibly quiet, and requires a scenic walk to
  access.

By the time you reach the Costa Brava, you will have already had your fill of
pure sunbathing in Santanyí. This leg of the trip is where you grab a cocktail,
hike the cliffs, eat world-class seafood, and explore the hidden corners of the
Mediterranean!
