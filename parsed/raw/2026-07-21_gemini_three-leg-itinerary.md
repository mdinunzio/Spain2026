Leg 1: Deià, Mallorca (4 Nights)
The Goal: Dramatic views, quiet luxury, and agricultural roots.

Since you are in the Tramuntana mountains, this is the absolute best leg to focus on historic estates and wine. The climate here is perfect for producing excellent dry, fruit-forward reds like Syrah and Manto Negro blends.

The Olive Grove Visit: Take a 15-minute drive to the neighboring village of Valldemossa to visit the Son Moragues estate. This is a meticulously restored, centuries-old olive grove. You can book a private, guided walk through the ancient terraces followed by a tasting of their organic, cold-pressed oils paired with local produce.

The Winery Trip: Spend an afternoon driving down into the central plains to the Binissalem wine region. Bodega Ribas offers intimate tastings in a stunning 18th-century courtyard.

The Dining: Focus on elevated, romantic dinners here. Places like El Olivo offer breathtaking sunset views and menus built around fresh, vibrant local vegetables and Mediterranean fish, entirely free of heavy, red-meat-centric dishes.

Leg 2: Santanyí, Mallorca (3 Nights)
The Goal: Pristine water, soft sand, and local culture.

This short stint on the east coast is dedicated to the ultimate, lazy beach days and soaking up the lively village atmosphere.

The Town Market: Santanyí hosts one of the most famous and beautiful open-air markets on the island every Wednesday and Saturday morning. The entire village center shuts down to traffic and fills with stalls selling local ceramics, tailored linen clothing, artisanal leather goods, and fresh produce. It is the perfect morning activity before heading to the water.

The Beaches: Hit the sand early. Cala Llombards and S'Amarador offer the exact white-sand, electric-blue water experience you want.

Leg 3: Llafranc, Costa Brava (7 Nights)
The Goal: Effortless beach access, coastal exploration, and world-class seafood.

Basing yourselves right on the water in Llafranc gives you the perfect launchpad for the more active, exploratory half of your trip, while still offering highly curated luxury.

The Vespa Rentals: Renting a vintage-style scooter here is brilliant. The roads connecting Llafranc, Calella de Palafrugell, and Begur are scenic and relatively easy to navigate. You can spend a day zipping along the coast, beach-hopping from Aiguablava to Sa Tuna, completely bypassing the hassle of finding a spot for a rental car.

The Boat Charter: Replicate your favorite Croatian experience by chartering a traditional wooden llaüt (or a sleek modern motorboat) out of the nearby Palamós or Llafranc marina. A skipper can take you north along the rugged, pine-fringed cliffs to drop anchor in secluded coves that are completely invisible from the road.

The Beach Clubs & Dining: While the Costa Brava is lighter on the massive "club" scene, it excels at barefoot luxury. Book a table right on the sand at Toc al Mar in Aiguablava for a massive lunch of whole, oak-wood-fired fish and grilled coastal vegetables—a spectacular way to eat beautifully clean, high-protein meals right on the water.
